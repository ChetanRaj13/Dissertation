R6: DIS-01 | Global Annual Data Centre Direct Water Consumption | 140 billion L direct water consumption globally in 2022 (P04, EXT-070, pdf p. 5 citing IEA 2024) | 313 to 764 billion L/year direct water consumption in 2025 (P05, EXT-088, pdf p. 1); >560 billion L in 2024 (P10, EXT-155, pdf p. 3; P07, EXT-195, pdf p. 7 citing IEA 2025) | IEA 2024 used a static top-down intensity factor (0.39 L/kWh) across global electricity demand (360 TWh); Rosa et al. (P05) developed a bottom-up facility model allocating 7,364 data centres to local wet-bulb temperatures and climate-dependent cooling hours, yielding 2.2x to 5.5x higher consumption. | Top-down global intensity benchmarks severely underestimate real-world evaporative cooling demand by ignoring local climate extremes and peak summer temperatures.
R7: DIS-02 | Direct Operational WUEsite Benchmarks Across Facilities | Microsoft operational direct WUE = 0.49 L/kWh (P03, EXT-048, pdf p. 2); AWS direct site WUE = 0.12 L/kWh (P14, PRS-024, pdf p. 2) | Google average direct WUE = 1.04 L/kWh (P04, EXT-075, pdf p. 7); Equinix direct WUE = 0.91 L/kWh (P14, PRS-024, pdf p. 2); Masanet median WUEsite = 0.95 L/kWh (P06, EXT-114, pdf p. 12) | Conflation of system boundaries (withdrawal vs consumption), distinct climate zones of data centre fleets, and differing cooling architectures (direct evaporative vs chilled water vs air-cooled chillers). | A single universal WUE benchmark cannot be applied across operators or geographies without adjusting for local wet-bulb temperature, economizer hours, and reporting boundary.
R8: DIS-03 | Share of Data Centres Located in Water-Stressed Areas | 20% of US data centre direct water from stressed subbasins (P02, EXT-022, pdf p. 3 using AWARE); 25% of global data centres face water gaps (P05, EXT-082, pdf p. 1) | 32% of US/global facilities in high/extremely high stress (P09, EXT-147, pdf p. 3); 41% of Asian data centres in extremely high stress (P08, EXT-181, pdf p. 2); 52% of global hubs in water-stressed regions (P10, EXT-158, pdf p. 3) | Differences in geographic scope (US vs Global vs Asia), differing hydrological stress indices (ISO 14046 AWARE vs monthly water gaps vs WRI Aqueduct BWS), and varying stress thresholds (40% vs 80%). | When evaluated using identical stress metrics, Asian and Indian data centres face nearly double the extreme water stress exposure of North American facilities.
R9: DIS-04 | System Boundary Accounting: Water Withdrawal vs. Water Consumption | Consumption (evaporative loss from local watershed) is the essential metric for assessing hydrological resource depletion (P01, EXT-008, pdf p. 1; P02, EXT-026, pdf p. 4; P04, EXT-068, pdf p. 5; P06, EXT-106, pdf p. 1) | Corporate disclosures and utility reporting frequently measure Withdrawal (total water intake, including return flows), which is easier to meter but conflates non-consumptive cooling with net loss (P03, EXT-048, pdf p. 2; P09, EXT-133, pdf p. 2; P14, PRS-024, pdf p. 2) | Operators report withdrawal to align with municipal utility billing meters or claim water circularity, while hydrologists require consumption to evaluate local watershed mass balances. | Conflating withdrawal and consumption distorts water footprint evaluations by 20% to 50% for direct cooling and up to 500% for hydroelectric generation.

R14: GAP-01 | Absence of Empirical Facility-Level Water Telemetry in India and Global South | P01, P02, P03, P04, P05, P06, P07, P08, P09, P10 | All quantitative facility models are US- or EU-centric; P07 is qualitative/stakeholder-based; P08 is regional text only; no empirical sensor telemetry exists for Indian data centres. | Forces Indian policymakers to rely on unverified corporate CSR claims or imported temperate-climate benchmarks that understate tropical cooling needs. | 
R15: GAP-02 | Omission of Seasonal and Diurnal Hydrological Stress Dynamics | P01, P02, P05, P06, P08, P09, P10 | Studies rely on annual baseline stress indices (WRI Aqueduct BWS or annual AWARE); only P05 models monthly water gaps, but no study integrates diurnal peak cooling during summer heatwaves. | In India's monsoon-dependent climate, annual averages mask severe 4-month pre-monsoon water deficits when data centre cooling demand peaks. | 
R16: GAP-03 | Lack of Disaggregation Across Water Source Types (Groundwater vs Municipal vs Tanker) | P01, P02, P04, P05, P06, P07, P09, P10 | Corporate disclosures aggregate water into a single volumetric number; only P07 qualitatively notes reliance on private water tankers and borewells in Chennai and Bengaluru. | Prevents direct attribution of data centre cooling to localized groundwater table depletion and aquifer over-extraction in Indian metropolitan fringes. | 
R17: GAP-04 | Total Omission of Scope 3 Embodied Water (Semiconductor Fabrication & Construction) | P01, P02, P03, P04, P05, P06, P07, P08, P09, P10 | P03 and P06 explicitly mention Scope 3 embodied water in their conceptual taxonomy (EXT-055, EXT-110), but neither quantifies it; no paper calculates embodied water in chip fabrication. | True lifecycle water footprint of AI expansion is substantially higher than operational metrics reflect, obscuring global water transfer embodied in AI accelerator imports. | 
R18: GAP-05 | Lack of Water Quality and Thermal Discharge Degradation Analysis | P01, P02, P05, P06, P07, P10 | Cooling tower blowdown water carries high mineral concentration and biocide chemicals; no paper evaluates downstream ecological or municipal wastewater treatment impacts. | Blowdown discharge in water-scarce Indian cities risks degrading municipal wastewater infrastructure and contaminating surrounding surface water bodies. | 
R19: 3. WEAKEST POINTS OF EVIDENCE BASE IN CORPUS |  |  |  |  |

R21: WKP-01 | Secondary Citation Cascades & Circular Evidence Loops | Multiple studies cite older benchmark figures (e.g. Shehabi et al. 2016 for US totals, or IEA 2024 for global aggregates) rather than generating fresh primary empirical data. | Creates an artificial impression of robust empirical consensus when the entire literature rests on a handful of foundational modeling papers. |  | 
R22: WKP-02 | Inferential Cooling Technology Modeling Without Ground-Truth Verification | Studies assign cooling systems to facilities via algorithmic proxy (e.g. P05 using climate regression) rather than verified ground-truth data centre equipment registers. | Macro-level regional aggregates are statistically robust, but individual facility allocations carry significant uncertainty. |  | 
R23: WKP-03 | Complete Opacity of Colocation and Wholesale Data Centres | Colocation facilities represent >50% of operational capacity globally and in India, yet disclose virtually zero public water metrics due to multi-tenant split incentives. | Literature is heavily skewed toward self-owned hyperscale campuses (Google, Microsoft, Meta), leaving the fastest-growing market segment completely unmonitored. |  |
